01

What WeldOps is

WeldOps is a dead-simple, phone-first app that helps small welding shops quote jobs, keep track of them, and get paid. It's for the solo welder working out of a truck and the owner of a 2 to 5 person shop. Today most of them run the business side on paper, texts, memory, and a QuickBooks file they dread opening. WeldOps replaces that with a few big buttons they can use with gloves on, between jobs, without calling anyone for help.

Quote a job
Price it right, text it over, turn a yes into a job with one tap.
Current jobs
What's going on now, with notes, photos, and the customer one tap away.
Get paid
Send the invoice, see who owes you, nudge the late ones.
Customers + My numbers
A simple phone book, and a plain answer to "did that job make money?"
02

What changed in V2

V1 (April 2026) planned a big product: 16 modules, four build phases, native apps. V2 cuts that down to what a welder would actually open every day, and fixes a few things V1 got wrong.

What we kept and sharpened
Slimmed to 5 functions: quote, jobs, get paid, customers, my numbers
Web app first, no App Store: saves to the home screen like a real app, updates instantly
Focused on ICP 1 and 2: the solo operator and the growing shop owner
Corrected revenue math: V1 priced customers at about $99 a month by mistake. V2 uses $45.
Honest competition framing: welders do use general apps today. Our real rival is paper, texting, and "good enough."
What we dropped (for now)
Metal futures and price charts
Inventory and stock tracking
Compliance, cert tracking, and weld procedure libraries
Scheduling calendar and dispatch
Crew management and crew logins
Shop website builder

Dropped items live in the parking lot on the Product tab. Some may come back once real customers ask for them.

03

The #1 design rule

Our customers are skeptical of techEvery decision gets judged by one question: would a welder who hates apps still use this? If the answer is "maybe, once we explain it," the answer is no.
They've been burned
Most have tried an app that promised to save time and ended up as one more thing to manage. They quit fast and don't come back.
They're busy with real work
The app gets used in a truck cab, on a tailgate, or at 9pm at the kitchen table. Not at a desk.
Fewer features wins
Every button we add is one more thing to learn. When in doubt, leave it out and see if anyone asks.

Competition, honestly

V1 said nothing had been built for this market since 2014. That overstated it. Welders do use software today, just not software made with them in mind.

General trade apps
Jobber, Housecall Pro, and similar
Good products, built for HVAC, plumbing, and landscaping
More setup and more screens than a one-truck welder wants
Invoice apps and QuickBooks
Handle the bill, not the quote or the job
Welders retype the same info two or three times
QuickBooks feels like homework
The real competitor
Paper, texting, and "good enough"
Free, already in their pocket, no learning curve
We have to be easier than a notebook, not better than Jobber
Our angleSimpler than anything else they could pick, and built around how welders quote: hours, a rate, the steel and consumables, a markup. Nothing to set up before the first quote goes out.

02

Core values

Both profiles share the same outlook, whatever the shop size. Everything WeldOps says and does should fit it.

What they value
Self-reliance: they built something with their hands, by themselves
No BS: they can smell a sales pitch from a mile away
Competence over credentials: they respect skill, not titles
Loyalty: once they trust something, they stick with it
Practicality: "does it work?" comes before "is it pretty?"
Pride in craft: the work itself is the identity
Skepticism of tech: burned before by tools that overpromised
Work ethic: 5am starts, Saturday jobs, no complaints
Branding approach: values, not symbols

This audience skews conservative and trades-proud. That's who they are, not something to market at them. Flag-waving on software reads as a pitch and sets off the exact cynicism we need to avoid.

Look at how Snap-on, DeWalt, and Lincoln Electric do it. None of them wave flags. They show the tool doing real work. The authenticity is the brand.

03

Voice & tone

Four pillars for how WeldOps talks everywhere: the website, the buttons in the app, error messages, and cold emails.

Pillar 1: Straight talk
Direct, no decoration, no passive voice. If it can be said in 8 words, don't use 20. No buzzwords. No corporate speak. Every sentence earns its place.
Pillar 2: Respect the craft
Never talk down to users. The audience is highly skilled and proud. WeldOps treats them as experts who deserve tools that work as well as theirs do, not beginners who need to be taught.
Pillar 3: Show, don't claim
This audience has been burned by overpromised tools. Trust comes from specifics and proof, not superlatives. Never "the best." Say what it does, how fast, and what it saves.
Pillar 4: Built by people who get it
Copy should sound like it came from someone who has spent time in a shop. Use trade words correctly. Don't overdo it. Earned, not performed.
Say this
"Quit losing track of jobs."
Not this
"Leverage mobile-first technology to optimize workflow management."
Say this
"Know if you're making money on every job."
Not this
"Gain comprehensive financial visibility across your project portfolio."
Say this
"You already know how to run a quality job. WeldOps makes sure the business side keeps up with you."
Not this
"WeldOps makes it easy for tradespeople who may not have a business background."
Say this (in the app)
"Quote sent. We'll let you know when they open it."
Not this
"Your estimate has been successfully transmitted to the client portal."
Words we useWords we avoid
Shop, job, crew, quote, invoice, track, see, know, runSolution, platform, ecosystem, leverage, optimize, streamline
Simple, fast, real, clear, built for weldersRevolutionary, game-changing, best-in-class, purpose-built for the vertical
Money, margin, profit, time savedROI maximization, value-add, strategic alignment

03

What each button does

One quote becomes one job becomes one invoice. Nothing gets typed twice.

Quote a jobPrice it, send it, win it
  • Pick the customer (or add a new one on the spot)
  • Describe the job in a line or two
  • Hours × rate for labor
  • Tap saved materials from My price list, easy-to-edit presets for steel, rod, gas, and so on
  • Markup and tax filled in already, with a live total as you go
  • Send it by text using the phone's own Messages app
  • When the customer says yes, one tap turns it into a job
Current jobsWhat's going on now
  • A plain list of the jobs in progress
  • Call, text, or get directions to the customer with one tap
  • Add notes and photos as the job goes
  • "Mark done" goes straight to an invoice, with nothing retyped
Get paidTop priority: invoicing
  • Send the invoice by text or email
  • The customer can tap a link to pay online
  • See who owes you and how late they are
  • Log cash, check, card, Venmo, or Zelle in two taps
  • Send a friendly reminder text when something's overdue
CustomersA simple phone book
  • Name, phone, email, address. That's it.
  • Tapping call, text, email, or directions opens the phone's own Phone, Messages, Mail, or Maps app
  • Each customer shows their history of quotes, jobs, and invoices
My numbersThree plain answers
  • Money in this month
  • Money owed to you
  • Which jobs actually made money: what you charged minus materials, and what you earned per hour
The flow in one line
Quote → customer says yes → job → Mark done → invoice → paid → shows up in My numbers.
04

Price nudges

Steel prices move. Welders who quote off last spring's prices lose money without noticing. So the app keeps a quiet eye on it.

Every preset in My price list remembers when its price was last updated. If a price is stale (about 90 days or older), the app gently flags it when you use it in a quote:

Your steel prices are 4 months old. Steel is up about 3% since then. Worth a call to your supplier?
Update pricesNot now
How it works, honestly
The trend comes from free monthly government producer price data
It's a general direction for the category, not the price at their supplier
It nudges. It doesn't promise to lock in prices or predict them.
One tap to dismiss. No nagging.
05

Bookkeeping stance

We do invoicing, not bookkeepingWeldOps tracks quotes, jobs, invoices, and payments. It doesn't try to be QuickBooks.
"Send to my accountant"
Exports invoices and payments as a spreadsheet and sends it wherever the user wants through the phone's share menu: their accountant's email, their own email, Google Drive, and so on. Shops that do their own books just send it to themselves.
QuickBooks sync
Offered as a very subtle, optional setting for shops that already live in QuickBooks. Not a main button, and not something we push.
06

How it ships

A web app that saves to the phone's home screen and opens like a real app. Welders get the same icon and the same full-screen feel, without the App Store.

No App Store
No approval wait, no app store fees, and every update shows up right away.
Texts from their phone
Quotes and reminders go out through the user's own Messages app. No texting service, no carrier registration, no per-text cost.
Voice notes for free
Hate typing? Use the microphone button already on the phone keyboard.
Real phone apps
Call, email, and directions hand off to Phone, Mail, and Maps.

Questions

What's the catch with "everything included"?
There isn't one. You pay $29 for yourself and $10 for each extra person. Every feature is in there for everyone.
Do I need QuickBooks?
No. WeldOps handles quotes, jobs, invoices, and payments on its own. If you or your accountant want the numbers, tap "Send to my accountant" and it goes out as a spreadsheet.
How do I cancel?
From inside the app. No phone call, no retention pitch.

01

The two tests

1

Use the clickable demo as the research and sales tool

Show it to 10 to 15 welders before building the real thing. Hand them the phone, ask them to quote a job they did last week, and stay quiet. Watch where they get stuck. Every stuck spot is a screen to fix.

Did they find "Quote a job" without being told?
Did they tap Help? Where?
What did they say out loud? Write down their exact words for the website.
At the end, ask: "Would you pay $29 a month for this?" Then watch their face.
2

The 10 paying welders test

Once the real app works, get 10 welders to pay $29 a month. Not free users, not "I'd probably use it." Paying. If 10 will pay and keep paying, we have something. If we can't get 10, we stop or change course before spending real money.

02

Where welders are

Trust travels by word of mouth in this trade. The best channels borrow trust from someone welders already listen to.

Welding YouTubers and Facebook groups
Where welders already hang out and swap advice. A real review or a demo in a group post beats any ad.
Local metal supply houses
Welders visit their supplier every week. A supplier recommending the app is a trust shortcut. A counter card or flyer costs almost nothing to test.
r/welding
Blunt feedback, fast. Good for demo testers. Show up as a person asking questions, not a brand posting ads.
Regional AWS chapter events
Face to face with shop owners. A good place to hand someone the demo on a phone.
